---
name: keeps-color
description: Adjust a single photograph non-destructively with Keeps local darktable tools; inspect each rendered candidate before selecting the final recipe.
---

Use the supplied Keeps MCP tools for this photo. Start with inspect_photo and get_recipe. The preview is rendered from the supplied source, which may be RAW or a rendered image such as JPEG or HEIF. Follow supportedAdjustments returned by inspect_photo; display-referred images currently support only exposureEV and saturation. Leave other recipe values unchanged. Geometric masks require supportsLocalMasks; semantic raster masks require supportsSemanticMasks. Do not seek a camera JPEG, matching curve, or reference histogram.

Identify the subject, lighting intent, exposure imbalance, and color cast from the image. For portraits, inspect the face with preview_region before editing and before selection. A face that remains difficult to see is not a successful natural portrait correction just because the background is preserved. When a person needs separate correction, prefer the person semantic mask if inspect_photo lists it. An ellipse is only a geometric exposure region and may affect background inside it; use one only when that spill is intentional and visually acceptable. Preserve the scene's lighting character and plausible skin color. Do not brighten every shadow or force every highlight to gray. Global exposure affects the whole scene. For person, background, or sky adjustments call generate_mask with that kind, inspect its returned white-on-black coverage, then use the returned maskID in a raster local adjustment. Background means everything outside detected foreground subjects, not simply the complement of a person. Never replace missing/failed segmentation with an ellipse or gradient while calling it a person, background, or sky mask. Inspect hair, horizons, buildings, and excluded areas; the small sky model can confuse building detail or textured clouds. If segmentation is not convincing, avoid the local correction and report the issue.

set_adjustments takes absolute values. Start from a named parent candidate; retain its ID for comparisons. whiteBalanceRGB multiplies the source's RGB white balance coefficients, rather than specifying Kelvin. contrast and skew control sigmoid. saturation=1 is neutral. Local masks replace the whole localAdjustments array. Ellipse centers are measured from the TOP LEFT and use normalized raw image width and height; radii and feather use the shorter raw edge. Ellipse feather extends OUTWARD from the inner radius: include that extra width when checking spill onto the background, and use smaller inner radii if the feather should end at the intended boundary. For gradients, rotation 0 affects the TOP, 90 the LEFT, -90 the RIGHT, and 180 the BOTTOM. The anchor is the 50% line; compression is HALF the complete transition width divided by the image DIAGONAL. A sky gradient crosses people, trees and buildings too; it does not follow the sky boundary. Do not claim either shape is a semantic subject/sky mask. Use only mask types listed by inspect_photo: semantic raster masks handle source orientation automatically, while geometric masks remain limited to orientation 1.

After each adjustment, call render_preview and assess the actual image: subject visibility, skin, clipping, color cast, halos, and unnatural local transitions. For every local adjustment, use preview_region on the same boundary region of the parent and edited candidate, including surrounding background, to check spill, halos and the transition direction; a face-only crop is insufficient. Reduce or remove the mask if the boundary looks artificial. Use compare_candidates to compare the best result with the initial preview or another candidate. Do not assume larger numeric changes improve the photo. You have at most four candidate adjustments; prefer one coherent correction per round and stop early when further changes would be speculative.

Every run, including a refinement, must produce and render a visibly adjusted alternative to the original and the starting candidate. Make at least one meaningful supported adjustment; do not return the baseline, iteration-base, an identical recipe, or a no-op as the result. Even if you prefer the original, provide the best adjusted alternative and explain your reservations in the Chinese reason. The user decides which version to adopt. Never decline to grade because the original is already good.

Call select_candidate only after reviewing that candidate's image. It renders the full-size local result; it does not publish to NAS. If the result is not convincing, return needs_review with the best adjusted, rendered candidate ID and concrete remaining problems. Never return unchanged. Do not invoke shell commands, change files yourself, or describe an unrendered candidate as successful.
